Response to the Second Referee Report -- DT18474/Kim

We thank the Referee for the careful second reading and for the endorsement
of publication.  We are glad the treatment of telescope optics now meets the
concern raised in the first report.

The Referee identifies four places where "reads" is used actively in a way
that could confuse.  The count is exact: these are the only four active uses
of that construction in the manuscript.  (The remaining occurrences of "read"
-- "Eq. (11) reads", "the weights read as fractions", "foam is read off the
line shape", "best read as an order-of-magnitude exclusion" -- are different
constructions that the objection does not touch, and we have left them
alone.)  All four have been changed.  They are marked in the accompanying
changes-marked copy of the manuscript; no other text has been altered.

1. Introduction, fifth paragraph, first sentence.

   "References [12,13] read those data more strongly"
   -> "References [12,13] advance a stronger interpretation of those data"

   We have adopted "interpretation" rather than the Referee's "interpret more
   comprehensively", because the point we intend is that those authors draw a
   stronger conclusion from the same data, not that they analyse a larger
   body of it.  We hope this meets the Referee's intent.

2. Introduction, same paragraph.

   "and read the resulting halo as attributable to foam"
   -> "and interpret the resulting halo as attributable to foam"

   Adopted exactly as suggested.

3. Section II A, first paragraph.

   "which a carrier reads as a phase jitter"
   -> "which a carrier converts into a phase jitter"

4. Section II A, third paragraph.

   "a monochromatic carrier ... reads the same delay as a phase jitter"
   -> "a monochromatic carrier ... converts the same delay into a phase
      jitter"

   Items 3 and 4 are the same physical statement, made informally in the
   first paragraph and precisely at Eq. (4), so we have given them a single
   verb rather than treating them separately.

   We have used "converts into" in place of the Referee's suggested
   "expresses" and "feels".  Our reason concerns item 4 in particular.  The
   delay dt is achromatic -- identical at every frequency, which is what "the
   same delay" in that sentence is carrying -- and what differs between
   frequencies is only the phase it produces, dphi = omega_0 dt.  "Feels"
   would suggest that the delay itself differs from photon to photon, which
   would sit awkwardly with the chromatic mechanism described three
   paragraphs earlier, where the achromaticity of the delay is precisely what
   makes the bluest photons the most blurred.  "Converts into" names the
   operation the equation performs and keeps delay and phase distinct.  We
   note the Referee allowed that "reads" may be standard usage here; we have
   changed it nonetheless, since the clearer phrasing costs nothing.

We are grateful to the Referee for both reports, which have improved the
paper considerably.
